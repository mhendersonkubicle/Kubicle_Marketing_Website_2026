Understanding Artificial Intelligence in the Workplace: Why It Matters for Business Leaders

AI Literacy 21 Jul 2023 6 min read

Before we delve into leveraging artificial intelligence in the workplace as a competitive differentiator, it’s important to recognise that AI has existed for quite some time. Since 1956, in fact.

And yet, it’s only in recent years that we have seen AI become a household name. Nowadays, wherever you look, AI’s influence is evident. It is now part of data assessments, creative projects and more, finding its way into every business department. This widespread adoption is transforming hiring strategies, upskilling plans and how organisations build capability across teams.

Understanding the impact of AI is no longer optional for leaders. It is now a fundamental requirement for anyone responsible for organisational performance, workforce readiness or long-term strategy.

Here, we want to give you an introduction to AI and an overview of the role it can have in your business.

What Is Artificial Intelligence?

While ideas relating to artificial intelligence have been discussed for centuries the Greeks even imagined early forms of robotic reasoning. The term itself was officially coined in 1956 at Dartmouth College. There, scientists introduced the concept of using algorithms to enable machines to learn from data.

Generative AI emerged much later and differs from earlier AI systems. Unlike traditional AI, which follows predetermined rules, Generative AI can create new content such as audio, art and text. Its capability stems from neural network models known as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), proposed by Ian Goodfellow and colleagues in 2014.

The growth of Generative AI has been remarkable. ChatGPT, for example, has over 800 million weekly active users as of October 2025. According to Statistica, the market size of Generative AI is projected to reach £59bn in 2025 and £400bn by 2031.

With this acceleration, artificial intelligence in the workplace has moved from a future concept to an operational reality. Organisations are now adopting these technologies to strengthen productivity, improve decision making and support employees across departments.

Unleashing Business Potential

As discussed, AI holds the potential to unlock unprecedented opportunities for businesses. By harnessing AI technologies, organisations can automate routine tasks, optimise processes and make data driven decisions with greater accuracy and speed.

Enhancing the Customer Experience

Optimising the customer experience has become a key differentiator in today’s business landscape.

When used correctly, AI powered solutions such as chatbots and personalised recommendations can transform customer service. They provide real time support, tailored product suggestions and streamlined interactions.

But misuse can have the opposite effect. Poorly designed or overused AI systems can frustrate customers and damage your brand. Understanding artificial intelligence in the workplace equips leaders to set clear standards so AI enhances customer experience rather than diminishing it. Navigating Ethical and Legal Considerations

AI raises important ethical and legal considerations as it becomes more deeply integrated into workplace systems.

Understanding AI helps leaders manage risks relating to:

  • Data privacy
  • Security
  • Algorithmic bias
  • Transparency
  • Responsible use of automated tools

Building strong governance ensures AI adoption aligns with ethical expectations and regulatory requirements. It also strengthens trust among employees, customers and stakeholders.
